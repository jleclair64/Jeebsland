# Jeebsland

Unreal Engine 5.8 C++ project (Windows). Game code lives in `Source/Jeebsland/`. Most of the world (forest, village, castle, pond, etc.) is built procedurally in C++ actors rather than placed assets.

- Default game mode: `KnightGameMode` (set in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`); the world is built by `KnightGameState`. Map: `/Game/Field`.
- The UE template code and content (Variant_*, Characters, ThirdPerson, LevelPrototyping, template game mode / controller) were deleted 2026-10-12. `AJeebslandCharacter` stays: it is the knight's base class (camera boom and camera). StateTree plugins are off.
- `Game Updates.txt` is the owner's private notes. Don't read or act on it; requests come through chat.

## Art direction (owner, 2026-11-05; applies to every design choice)

This is permanent and goes into every design from now on.

- **World, weapons and items:** a Breath of the Wild meets Overwatch look. Simple, but smooth and detailed. Focus on good colours and textures, not realism, but still pretty and natural. Think of how the forests of Fortnite look, the medieval towns in Overwatch and the landscape of Breath of the Wild.
- **Items** (weapons, tools, potions and so on): a mash-up of Breath of the Wild, Overwatch, Fortnite, Skyrim and RuneScape, slightly cartoonish, an HD RuneScape feel. Good detail, smooth, not too small. In practice (owner, same day, after seeing both ends and sending reference pictures): "not quite realistic but a tiny bit cartoonish". Sleek, natural proportions only a little bolder than real life, crisp bevels, hand-painted textures, rich clean colours, polished wood and dark steel, no rust or dirt. Too realistic was the first thin, weathered set; too cartoonish was fat, toy-like, banana-shaped forms. Wood is rich brown with just a touch of painted grain and a little natural variance: not yellow or orange, no drastic colour change along it. **`Docs/ItemStyleGuide.md` is the recipe (the process, Meshy settings, prompt wording, proportions, and the approved pictures in `Docs/ItemStyle/`): read it before designing any item, so the balance between cartoonish and realistic never has to be found again.**
- **Armour** is the exception: it may lean more towards realism.

Say this in every Meshy prompt and check every new model, material and building against it.

## Model and effort

The owner wants a recommendation on model and effort for each request, to balance quality against token use. Before executing any large task (or a batch of tasks), always suggest the most optimal model and effort first. "Optimal" means balancing token usage while still getting a great result that won't need rework. For a mixed batch, say which items suit which model. For small requests, one line is enough, and only if it differs from the current setting. Don't switch unless asked.
- Sonnet 5.5, low or medium effort: tweaks to values, colors, sizes or positions; removing things; small single-file changes.
- Sonnet 5.5, high effort: moderate features or multi-file changes along existing patterns.
- Opus 5.5, high effort: new systems (skills UI, enemies, combat, dungeons), tricky visual or rendering bugs, anything Sonnet failed at.

## Ending a session

When the owner says goodbye, goodnight or anything similar, don't just sign off: first ask if they'd like you to commit and refresh the "Current state" notes below for their next chat. If they say yes, update that section (what was just done, what's unfinished, known issues, decisions, anything they want carried over), remove stale lines, keep it to about a screen, and commit. In the same question, also ask whether to repackage the game (only if code or content changed since the last package; then make `Packaged/Jeebsland.zip` without the `.pdb`, and remind them to restart the worlds with `Tools/HostWorlds.bat` and send friends the new zip).

How the suit's animator works (`FKnightAnimator`, 2026-11-05):
- Code writes the bones of a `UPoseableMeshComponent`; clips are read at load as dense frames x bones from `Content/Data/KnightClips.bin` (list `ClipNames`). Game state chooses a key (`ChooseAction`; the old rules, timers and hit windows still decide everything) and each key change starts an inertial blend (a decaying offset from the old pose, so no pop and no cross-fade), with the right hand's rate capped so the blade doesn't whip.
- Layers: locomotion (9 clips by direction and speed on a shared, distance-based phase), overlays from the waist (crouch, block, bow), then actions (one-shots, time-warped so their hit and follow-through land on the game's own hit windows).
- `Content/Data/KnightMoves.csv` holds each move's clip, cut points (a tool swing's ClipHit is the top of the wind-up and ClipFollow the blow; sit and slide play to ClipHit and hold), legs mode (`LegsFull` 2 full, 1 by speed, 0 upper only), blend time, `HipLock` (removes a clip's own forward hip travel where the capsule does the moving) and `SpineKeep` (how much of the chest's turn stays with the stance, more while running, so a slash doesn't turn his back on the target). No build needed; for falls `ClipHit` is seconds shown, for jump, land and hits it is playback rate.
- `PlaceGear` moves `WristR`, `ElbowL` and `UpperPivot` onto the clip's bones so the weapon, trail and hit boxes follow it. `SolveGear` measures how the weapon, shield, bow and quiver sit on the bones, once, at load.
- Last step each frame, after the blend: the hips rise if a boot would sink (`Sole`), and `KeepTipUp` turns the wrist to keep the sword's tip at least 5 cm off the floor.
- After a fight he stands on guard (`Idle_SS`, 4 s, `CombatT`) so sword clips start and end in the same stance, then eases back to `Rest`.
- Adding a clip: download FBX from Mixamo (on its stock Y Bot) into `Art/Knight Chainmail/Clips`, run `Tools/ImportKnightClips.ps1` with the editor closed (`knight_clip_retarget.py` in Blender, `knight_clip_import.py` in Unreal), add the name to `ClipNames` and a CSV row; the editor rebakes `KnightClips.bin` at its next run, so commit that file. `knight_suit_import.py` wipes `/Game/NewSuit`, so re-import the clips and the bare knight after any suit import.
- Test pictures: `-ShotMove=...`, `-SuitWalk=<dir>_<share>_<from>_<for>`, `-SuitSprint`, `-SuitDo=roll<deg>|stun<hundredths>|knock|drop|crouch`, `-SuitClip=` / `-SuitClipAt=`, `-SuitGallery`, `-SuitLogClips`, `-LabNoDummies`, `-ShotSeconds=N`. Log: JEEBANIM (key, worst per-frame bone change, feet, hip, foot slide, sword tip), JEEBPATH (each move's hand, foot and hip paths).

## Climbing and the mantle (2026-11-12; Opus agent, not yet seen by the owner)

- Free climbing like Breath of the Wild and a Fortnite-style mantle are custom movement modes in the knight's own movement component, `UKnightMovement` (KnightMovement.h/.cpp; set on the knight through `AJeebslandCharacter`/`AKnightCharacter`'s FObjectInitializer constructors). They run inside Unreal's movement prediction: the player's machine predicts, the server runs the same moves from the same inputs (the keys' direction, Space, Ctrl) and corrects position, mode, stamina and the scripted move (`FKnightMoveResponse` carries the climbing state in corrections). Never the player's word for where he is. Modes (`JeebClimb::EMode`): 1 on the rock, 2 the climb jump, 3 the ledge climb-up, 4 mantle over, 5 mantle onto. The ladder is separate and unchanged (`bClimbing`, RepMoveFlags bit 16).
- What can be climbed (`Climbable`): a component tagged `JeebClimb` (the world's ground in WorldTerrain.cpp, the original square in GrassField.cpp, MoveLab's rock), only where too steep to walk and at most 15 degrees overhanging. Never buildings, trees, fences, walls, gear, mining rocks or Oak Hill's ground (untagged). Grabbing: walk or jump into it until he touches it (within 50 degrees of square on; the probes run only after a move that touched tagged rock, `HandleImpact`, so a busy server pays nothing for everyone else), not holding Ctrl, not right after letting go (until he lands), not with an emptied bar.
- Mantle: a component tagged `JeebMantle` (MoveLab's fences and stone block; real fences later) whose top is `MantleMin`..`MantleMax` above his feet. Space by one, or running into one in the air. Thin ones he goes over to the ground beyond, deep ones he climbs onto. Just a movement: no stamina, no XP.
- Controls: on the rock W/S up and down, A/D sideways, diagonals; Space the climb jump the way the keys point (`ClimbJumpDist` 100 cm in `ClimbJumpTime` 0.6 s for `ClimbJumpCost` 0.2 of the bar; less left than that, Space does nothing); Ctrl lets go. At the top he climbs up onto the ledge by himself.
- Climbing is a skill: a full bar lasts `AKnightCharacter::ClimbSeconds(level)` climbing upward (Tuning.ini [Climb] ClimbAt1 5 s, ClimbAt10 17.5, ClimbAt30 23, ClimbAt99 30, straight lines between; the skills window says it). Hanging still drains 1/5 as fast (`ClimbHangShare`), sideways 2/3 (`ClimbSideShare`), down half (`ClimbDownShare`), a diagonal mixes its parts. Emptied: he falls (no fall damage, owner) and can't grab again until the bar has refilled to full on the ground. Climbing XP: `ClimbXPPerMetre` for new height above the best on that grab (server). All numbers in Tuning.ini [Climb].
- Owner-approved (2026-11-12), keep as they are: 4 Climbing XP per metre of new height, climbing down drains half as fast, an emptied bar refills fully before he can grab again, Ctrl lets go and Space mantles.
- HUD: a green liquid-glass bar under the middle of the screen while climbing and while refilling (red and throbbing when emptied).
- Pose (KnightAnimatorClimb.cpp, keys 80-91): Mixamo clips WallHang, WallUp, WallDown, ShimmyL/R, HopUp/Down/L/R, LedgeUp, VaultOver, ClimbOnto (rows in KnightMoves.csv; for the loops ClipHit is cm per cycle, the stride follows distance climbed). On the rock the whole body tilts about the hips to lie along it and is set so the hands are on it; scripted moves keep the clip's path first and the capsule's by the end. Weapon and shield go on his back on the rock, in the climb-up and in both mantles (`ClimbStowed`, any climb mode), so the hands are free for the fence or the block. What is on his back follows the back's line, hips to neck (`BackLineW`), and moves in toward him as he goes flat over a ledge (`StowPull`, 18 cm at flat; test `-StowPull=<cm>`). The vault is Mixamo's VaultTwo cut to its knee tuck (0-0.34 s; later it leans right back) and tipped 24 degrees forward about the hips from mid-vault to the landing (`VaultTipDeg` in KnightAnimatorClimb.cpp) so he drops forward onto his feet instead of sitting, with both hands pressed onto the fence top while he goes over (`PlantHands`, the edge from `PlanMantle`: `ActEdge`, others' machines find it as the mantle starts; log JEEBVAULT). After the ledge pull-up and a climb onto he stands on top for `MantleStandTime` 0.25 s before he walks on (inside the move: `ScriptedHold`; the pose goes to rest at once and rises out of the crouch). On the rock each hand and boot rests on the surface traced under it, 1.5 cm off, never in it (`ClimbIK`: the gauntlet to the fingertips and the boot to the toe cap with their thickness, two-bone reaches, the body eased off the rock for the hands; limbs swinging to the next hold are left alone; log JEEBCLIMBIK, before and after). Every way off the wall eases out (`WallExitBlend`: 0.6 s from the ledge and block, 0.4 the vault, 0.5 letting go), held 0.4 s so a second key change can't cut it short.
- Test: MoveLab rock at X 1150-2000, Y -1080..-300 (west face sheer toward the start, south 70, north 55, east 40 walkable), fences at X 1000 (Y 300-600 1 m, 620-900 1.35 m), block at (1000, 1150). Switches `-ClimbKeys=<up>_<right>_<from>_<for>[x...]` (hundredths), `-ClimbDo=jump<at>|letgo<at>|space<at>`; logs JEEBCLIMB, JEEBMANTLE, JEEBCLIMBPOSE (hands and feet to the rock, slip), JEEBCLIMBCLIP.

## Parallel agents for every multi-part request (owner, 2026-11-11; standing rule, never wait to be reminded)

When a request has several independent parts, split them across background agents from the START of the turn, scaled to the size (a quick fix needs none; Opus for motion, visuals and new systems, Sonnet for data and colour tweaks). Each agent gets its own files and a full brief (the owner's words, picture paths, files it may and may not touch). Agents run their own reviewers but do not commit; the main session keeps the work that touches the most shared files, runs shared rebuilds once (e.g. ImportNewSuit, then clips, then the bare knight), and commits everything together.

Each agent works in its own project copy (owner, 2026-11-13, "let's do it"; starting with the batch after that night's):
- `bash Tools/AgentCopy.sh new <agent>` makes `../JeebsAgents/<agent>` on branch `agent/<agent>` from main's latest commit, with the big git-ignored assets linked in (Content/Filler, Art originals); `remove <agent>` unlinks them first, then deletes the copy (only once merged). Commit everything in the main folder before making copies, or the copies start without it.
- The agent edits, builds (Build.bat with the copy's .uproject) and runs test pictures only in its copy, and commits to its own branch (never pushes). Another agent's half-finished code can never break its build.
- `Tools/uqueue.sh` shares the machine: at most 3 Unreal processes at once (6 cores, 32 GB, 8 GB GPU; a fourth only slows the others), first come first served, one build at a time per copy, `solo` for frame-rate timing (holds every slot). Same rules as below: run it in the background, release the moment Unreal exits, `-RenderOffScreen`, `-abslog=<copy>/Saved/Logs/<agent>.log`.
- A copy's first build compiles the whole game module (about 10-20 minutes, ~5 GB); shaders come from the shared engine cache. Disk: about 8 GB a copy (116 GB free on 2026-11-13); remove copies after merging.
- The main session merges the branches into main, settles conflicts, runs the shared rebuilds once, checks the whole, commits and pushes. Unreal assets (.uasset, .umap) can't be merged: each asset folder has one owning agent per batch.
- Linked shared assets belong to the main folder: a copy rebuilds them only if its brief says so.

How agents shared Unreal in one folder (owner, 2026-11-13: "find a smarter/optimal way"; a free-for-all with one lock had five agents waiting while one held it 75 minutes; still the rule for work in the main folder):
- `Tools/ulock.sh` is a first-come-first-served queue. `build NAME` holds the exclusive build lock (`Saved/unreal.lock`) for builds, importers, material builds, the editor and packaging. `run NAME` holds one of two run slots for `-game` / test-picture runs, so two can go at once but never during a build. `release NAME` gives it back; `status` shows the queue. Waiting blocks, so agents run it in the background. Stale locks clear themselves when no Unreal process has run for 3 minutes; holders past 30 min (build) or 12 min (run) show OVERDUE.
- Every run: `-RenderOffScreen` (no window over the owner's games) and `-abslog=<repo>/Saved/Logs/<agent>.log` (two runs at once would share Jeebsland.log). GPU timing is measured alone.
- Hold only while Unreal runs: never while thinking, editing or waiting for reviewers.
- Batch per agent: finish every edit first (a build compiles everyone's code, so never leave a half-edit in a file), one build, then all pictures in as few runs as possible.
- A build failing in another agent's file: release and tell the coordinator at once with the error lines.
- Size the batch: at most about four agents that need Unreal at once; Blender, data, research and Meshy/Mixamo work can run beside them freely.

## Mixamo: use Claude in Chrome (2026-11-05)

Mixamo has no official API, so there is no connector or key. Use Claude in Chrome (`mcp__claude-in-chrome__*`, owner's choice), where downloads save straight to Downloads with no dialog (tested 2026-11-05). The owner is logged in there; their name shows at the top right. Open your own new tab and close it when done. `navigate` works with URLs like `https://www.mixamo.com/#/?page=1&query=breathing%20idle&type=Motion%2CMotionPack`.
- If `list_connected_browsers` is empty, ask the owner to open Chrome with the Claude extension. It only works while Chrome is running on this PC.
- The login can expire. Then ask the owner to log in again in Chrome; they use "Continue with Facebook". Never type their password, and never act on their Facebook account.
- Fallback only: the app's built-in browser is logged in too, but every download there opens a Windows Save dialog that only the owner can click (see below).
- Don't use unofficial Mixamo download scripts or `mixamo-mcp`: they rely on undocumented web behaviour, and it is unconfirmed that Adobe's terms allow them.
- Mixamo opens on whichever character was last chosen, but our clips are downloaded on its stock Y Bot (see "Adding a clip" under "Animation direction"). Check that the viewer's title says "... ON Y BOT" before downloading.
- Download settings (tested 2026-11-05 with Breathing Idle; `Tools/knight_clip_retarget.py` took it fine): FBX Binary, Without Skin (the dialog defaults to With Skin), 30 fps, keyframe reduction none. In Chrome the file lands in `%USERPROFILE%\Downloads` under the clip's name, with `(1)` added if that name is taken, so look for the newest file. This relies on Chrome's "Ask where to save each file" staying off. Rename it and move it to `Art/Knight Chainmail/Clips` only when it is really being added, because the importer reads every `.fbx` in that folder. Then follow the normal "Adding a clip" steps.
- Mixamo clips cost nothing, so sessions can download what a task needs without asking.
- In the built-in browser, the file stays a `.tmp` until the owner clicks Save, and the app has no setting to skip that dialog. The pane also refuses `navigate` while a page-opened pop-up is open in any tab; don't close a pop-up you didn't open.
