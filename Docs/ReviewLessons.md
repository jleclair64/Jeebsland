# Review lessons

Things the reviewers (and the owner) have caught before. Read the sections that match a change BEFORE building it, so the
first review round passes. Every time a reviewer FAILs something, add its `LESSON:` line here (one line, a general rule,
not the one-off fix). Keep each line short; merge lines that say the same thing.

## HUD and menus (visual-reviewer)
- Text inside a cell or tile is at least the item badges' size (scale 1.3 in a 50 px Armor cell) and has a dark edge (black, about 0.6 alpha, 1 px offset). (2026-11-02: the armour weight readout was too small and faint.)
- Warm colours (orange, red) on grey glass need high brightness and saturation, e.g. (1.0, 0.74, 0.3); muted ones read as brown-grey.
- A tile meant to read as a cell needs about 0.2 alpha; less looks like a gap.
- Centre text blocks vertically in their cell, and check the widest string still fits at the chosen scale.
- Always liquid glass (`Panel`, `Tile`, `Gauge`), text only through `HDText` / `HDTextSize`, icons with `Poly`.
- Pictures of the Tab windows: `-JeebShot -ShotPanel` (add `-ShotWear=<tier>` for a full suit); crop the panel 2x for the reviewer.
- (Tab map, 2026-11-13) Names over a map or other busy art sit on a dark pill at full opacity, beside (not on) what they name, with a gap from its marker; place them in order of importance and move or drop one that would cover another name, a box or the player's arrow. Test the final padded pill against every other name, every outline (the real rotated shape, not its bounds) and the arrow for every kind of name, keep fades short (a half-faded name on busy ground is unreadable), and check every zoom step.
- (Climbing, 2026-11-12) A HUD bar never sits at the screen's middle where the camera keeps the knight: place it clear of him and make it plainly different (colour, thickness) from the other bars.

## Poses, held and worn items (visual-reviewer)
- (Climbing, 2026-11-12) A move that crosses an obstacle shows a hand or foot on it at the contact moment, and stowed gear stays fixed to the chest bone through every pose, flips and drops included.
- (Climbing, round 2) Gear stowed on the back stays in contact with the back (a few cm at most) through every pose, horizontal ones included: follow the back's line, not one spine bone that the clip arches. A vault is judged on its contact moment: no hand or foot visibly on the obstacle while the body crosses is a flat dive and fails, whatever the clip is called.
- (Climbing, round 3) When a clip carries a held item (axe, sword), check that the item and the hands never take the place of the contact point (the rail or the block top): pick a clip whose hands land on the obstacle, or stow the item for the move.
- (Climbing, round 3) Gear frames fixed for one pose (the vertical climb) must be checked in every other pose of the same move (the flat ledge crawl): apply the back-lining to every key in the move, not just the main loop.
- (Climbing, round 4, owner) Hands and feet on a surface are measured by their real extent (wrist to fingertips, ankle to toe cap, with thickness) against the surface traced under each limb, never by the wrist or ankle bone to a plane: the bones read 6-10 cm off the rock while the gauntlets were 16 cm and the boots 20 cm inside it. A clip made on another wall needs per-limb IK onto the surface, and the close-ups that check it are side-on, like the owner's.
- (Climbing, round 4) Contact with an obstacle is proven from a camera that sees the hand and the obstacle together (side-on or from the front), never from behind or along the rail; a vault passes only when a picture shows each hand on the rail. A vault or mantle keeps the torso over the hips: a clip that sits or leans back reads as a hop or a fall.
- (Climbing, round 4b) A log of 0 cm from a hand to its target is not proof: if the arms hang while the legs lift, it reads as a step-over. The hand must sit visibly on top of the rail in a picture. For any landing, check the last frames before he stands: feet planted under the hips, torso not low or reclined, and stowed gear still stowed.
- Pictures of a hand or grip change, or of a greyed HUD state, must be close-ups at the moment that matters (the peak of the swing, the end guard, the bar with the item disabled). A wide sheet of small frames can't prove a grip or a grey-out. Check the closing pose too: a condition on the state key that names the wrong key (the guard is key 2, not 0) is invisible in mid-swing.
- A held item must show its identifying part (neck, head, tip) past the hand, from the side, in every frame of the move. If the clip's hand leaves no room, offset the hand; don't hide the part. Measure the visible part against the fist's width (about 12 cm). After clearing a clip, re-check contact from the side: the item must still visibly connect to the hand.
- Check every worn item against the arm in every clip, and show what holds it on: a strap, a loop, a hook. Nothing floats beside the body.
- Check a bottle's every face in the hand, base included, and let the part that matters (the neck at the mouth) show past the fist.
- Keep the forward hand behind a crossbow's prod. A stand-in clip made for another object: check it against the held item's real shape.
- Size wall rows to an item's widest span, not its length. Check the raised shield's front for fingers.
- Measure clipping with `JEEBCLEAR` and sweep numbers until clear before taking pictures; never guess.
- Arms hang close to his sides at rest; make room by placing the item, not by moving the arm.
- Swords are really gripped: through the curl of the fingers, blade out past the thumb.
- Check every side an item can clip on (the quiver against arm, cape and fist; the bow and string against body and elbow).
- Judge a drawn bow's lean from a true side-on view with a level camera, never an oblique one, and add close crops of the bow fist on the grip and of the arrow where it passes the shoulder plate (2026-11-13).
- Trails follow the sunlight; nothing glows brightly at dusk.
- Variant pictures (styles, tiers, slots): check each differs from the last before sending; an identical picture means the test switch never reached the model (gear set by test code must also tell the knight to redraw: OnGearChanged).
- A board or picker that offers several styles shows the real models of each style, never shared placeholders, or the choices cannot be told apart.
- A board of items to browse is sized to read from where the player stands (life size or larger, big headers); check it with a picture from the start spot.

- Every armour style must show its tier: where a model's cloth takes no tint, give it its own cue (trim, belt or a wash) or say plainly that it doesn't.
- A very dark tier's icons need a backing or outline on the real glass panel, checked at actual size; a brightness floor is not enough, and icons must also be easy to tell from each other (a strong tonal gap between icon and backing). Two shiny tiers must differ in hue and polish, not just brightness.
- Fit a combined piece per body part (thigh, shin, foot), checked front and side against the whole figure: a widening that suits the thigh makes the feet look oversized.
- When a cut by position crosses thin or folded geometry (corners, flanges, collars), look closely at each place it crosses: tidy the ends so no teeth or slivers stay behind.
- When asked to blunt or round a point, compare the silhouette before and after from the side: a change you can only measure is not one the owner will see.
- Judge a new model's colour in the game, next to the same tier and in the SAME light (the same spot, worn the same way). A neutral Blender render shows nothing about the tint, and a suit in sunlight against one in shadow proves nothing.
- Fit armour to the owner's reference pictures by measuring both at the same height, side by side, and look at the raw model in plain clay first: it may already have the shape you are about to force. A dome's fullness shows in its width halfway up, not its overall size.
- Never shear a rounded plate to tilt it (a pad became a warped ramp, 2026-11-05): find which part really leans, usually what hangs below the rim, and move only that. Check a tilt's direction side-on against the picture: tipping a pad's bottom back drops its front.
- What sits under armour is deleted, not set deeper. What stays in a gap between two plates must end inside both, on every side and in every clip: look from behind too, and check rims at full swing, not just standing (a sleeve covered a bracer's rim only at the back of the elbow, and webbing at the armpit counted as arm by its weights).
- Keeping or dropping whole triangles leaves a saw-toothed edge. Where an edge can show, cut it with a plane and smooth it. Give anything tucked under a plate that plate's own weights near the rim, so the two can't part.
- Meshy models are split along texture seams and may have inner shells: weld before asking what is connected, and remove faces that face inward before reshaping. Reducing one to a few percent of its triangles across those seams shatters its picture into speckled shards: weld, reduce, then give it fresh UVs and bake the pictures from the full model (`bake=True` in prop_clean.py). Look at the reduced model textured, in a render where it fills the frame, before importing.
- (Building look, 2026-11-12) When flattening or denoising a texture, protect its thin dark separator lines (mortar, block and plank gaps) from the smoothing, in the colour and the normal map, and measure how many survive (a black top-hat count against the raw: about 60% had gone and walls read soft and pale). Remove baked shade from lightness only: keep each surface's painted chroma (hue and saturation) as it was, speckle aside, since the eye reads a surface's colour from its painted warm light, and stone went cold grey without it even with every group's average kept. Check the warm/cool balance of every colour group on the final in-game render (measure it), not only on texture averages.
- Judge armour in motion at full render scale or in Blender. The upscaler frays fast-moving edges at 60% and it reads as torn plate; test before believing either explanation (run the same frames at both scales).
- Check a new prop's lighting in the game before tuning its colour. Models imported with their own normals keep the old direction when a script turns the vertices, so they read dark and flat from every side; the bows and the potion did, and the wood was brightened to make up for it (2026-11-06). Turn the normals with the mesh.
- When a change tints items by tier, picture each item in a bright tier other than Tin (`-ShotKind=<n> -ShotKindTier=<tier>`): `-ShotKind` alone gives Tin, whose grey reads as "not tinted", and a colour that works on one weapon proves nothing about the others.
- For an item to "read as its tier's colour", about a third or more of what you see from the views that matter (held, from above and the side, and the weapon wall's angle) must take the tint, as whole fittings with a clear wood-against-metal contrast; small bands and caps read as spots of colour (crossbow and longbow, 2026-11-11). Judge it on the tiers that fade most (gold, silver) in sun and in shade. A mirror-shiny tint reflects the blue sky and turns gold olive, so keep part of its own colour in the diffuse. The wall shows a crossbow's underside, and `-ShotKind=` only gives tin, so a held top view of another tier needs its own switch.
- A held or hung item is judged from every side and against the body: the first belt flask hung 25 cm under the belt and looked like it was floating, and a dark ball in the hand hid that its liquid colour never showed.
- Don't judge a held item or a blade's clearance from tiny sheets: crop so the knight fills at least half the frame. Test films read the local save, so always pass `-ShotKind=`; a dark suit from the save can also hide a problem.
- A shield stowed on the back is checked from behind and both sides in every move that stows it, against the shoulders, head and cape. It lies flat, no higher than the shoulders and no wider than the torso, and the cape must not flare like a flag.
- Retake any test series that comes back mis-framed before reviewing it: check one frame's framing first. Check too that the film shows what you meant: in MoveLab a run straight ahead hits the wall before the pictures start, so eight identical frames were read as a stiff run. A straight-behind film needs another way; `-ShotFacing=-60` gives a rear three-quarter view.
- Judge a grip from a close side crop where the fist and handle are separate in silhouette; a foreshortened or dark-on-dark view can't pass or fail it. Check an orientation request in two perpendicular views, and quote the owner's words in the brief: "lines up with the near-vertical line" was once briefed as hanging straight down.
- Anything on his back follows the chest bone, so cloth that also follows it goes on past his waist like a board when he bends over: below the waist it has to hang. Check every swing frame from the side and from behind.
- Anything built in code to stand in for part of a skinned armour piece (a hand, a plate) must match the piece beside it: its colour on a plain material has to be darkened to match the suit's painted texture, in more than one tier, and it must be as wide as its neighbour. Check the join from at least two sides.
- Work out which way the owner's marked-up picture looks before reading it: a line drawn on a picture from above is a shield standing upright seen edge-on, not a shield tilted. Film from their angle, brief the reviewer on it, and judge a piece fixed to the arm by the angle of its plane to the forearm in that view.
- A tool or axe has a wide head where a sword has a thin point, and the sword's clips bring that end up by his cheek: check the helm's clearance in the guard and the recovery from a crop where the knight fills half the frame.
- Check a fist shape key against the owner's list one item at a time, each in a view where it can be seen alone: the thumb across the fingers (palm view), no light through the hand along the handle (end view), no gaps or points between the fingers (palm view). A finger curl that closes in cross-section does not prove the thumb or the spaces between the fingers moved (a low-poly thumb's weight caught only its five tip vertices, 2026-11-11).
- Every hand state that changed (fist and relaxed) needs an in-game close-up where the hand fills the frame (`-ShotEye=X_Y_Z -ShotFov=`). Wide pictures where the hand is cut off or 20 pixels across cannot pass a hand change.
- Judge a fist morph by where each digit ends up against the handle (the thumb over the fingers, every tip within about 0.5 cm of the curl), not by the shape closing in cross-section; colour the thumb in a test render to see where it went. Smooth the morph near the fingertips: a low-poly tip that only turns comes out as torn spikes. Measure light through the hand along the handle with rays, not by eye. Fit it against the real radius of the thickest handle it holds (WeaponDesign.h grips are 2.2-2.6 cm), not a thinner test rod: a handle cutting through the fingers reads as torn fingertips, and only an in-game close-up shows it.
- Check every item that should close a hand in a close-up at rest and in use (shield, crossbow, two-handed tools). A per-item fist rule can be on in code and still leave a flat hand where the clip turns the hand away from the item.
- Before handing in-game pictures of a mesh or morph change for review, prove they show the new asset: compare one crop with the previous round's, and check the loaded asset (for the bare hands, SK_NewSuitBare) is newer than the change. An import log alone does not prove the game used it.
- (Swimming, 2026-11-14) Judge a water pose from a camera just above the water, side-on, with the surface and the whole body in frame (`-ShotEye` at about the waterline + 60 cm, out over open water, clear of the reeds): an underwater or glary camera cannot show how much of him is out. A test film must end on the move it was meant to show, not on a death or respawn; check the log's mode lines before handing pictures over (an invisible template floor once kept him walking on the riverbed in every film).
- (Swimming, 2026-11-14) A prone swim must keep the helm and shoulders clearly above the water and read as a person facing a way: a flat breaststroke clip with the hips just under the surface puts the head at the waterline and the legs and belt gear on top (a floating suit of armour). Tip the body head up at the surface (Breath of the Wild) and log the head bone against the surface (JEEBSWIMPOSE head vs 96 + depth) before taking pictures; judge from frames where the whole body fits, big enough to read.

## Moves and movement (motion-reviewer)
- A request that depends on a line the owner drew (a flat cut's height, a trail's start) needs a sheet from the owner's own camera angle, with the trail and the cut's frames cropped large enough to see, next to the drawing.
- When a clip switch starts a wind-up from the previous cut's end pose, log the tip and hip height across the switch frame; over 80 cm in a frame, or 70 degrees or more of bone change, needs a longer blend or a later ClipStart before the sheet is shown.
- When a blend is lengthened, re-measure every contact number in the move: hips arriving late lift the blade. Judge a blend by its first frame's share, not its stated length. Anything raising a foot must ease in.
- Warp each stretch of a clip under about 1.6x, its tail included. Every clip swap blends, even within one mode. Never derive a heading from a near-vertical bone. Size a foot's lift so a dragged boot clearly steps.
- Read the foot-slide log across every key change. A move's legs mode must never change in a frame: the feet jump and the hand goes with them.
- Recovering from a lunge, the rear foot steps or stays; it never drags. Size a get-up's blend by how far the hips travel, and keep some of the glide while rising.
- Two-handed clips must release or stow the shield.
- Measure the blade against his own body on every clip with a blade (JEEBSELF: body, head, tip and hand ahead); reviewers once passed a jab that stabbed himself. A clip's name doesn't say which part is the cut: read its path.
- Check two saved frames after an attack returns to stance and size the out-blend from them. A follow-through the owner drew must finish on the side the cut ended before the hand heads to the guard.
- Parts kept from the pose underneath (an arm masked out of a clip) must come from a fixed pose, not whatever was playing; judge held poses from behind too.
- A clip whose feet leave the floor (the axe clip hops) reads as a jump when he should be standing: use `LegsFull` 0 for work done on the spot.
- Check every new clip's facing against the stance before wiring it in: Mixamo's ladder climber faces the opposite way from the rest. A knight's chest heading in the log (or the first frame showing his back) gives it away.
- Never read a tilted bone's heading through `Rotator().Yaw`: it flips 180 degrees past vertical. Anything carried by a heading (the cape) must fade it out as the bone tips up.
- A move's recovery must carry the hand and blade along one arc into the guard, and the clip must end early enough that the blend out (0.3 s or more) does the settling. Judge the last quarter of every attack sheet for the blade changing height more than once, or going out of sight.
- Tool and bow clips were made for an empty off hand, so every off-hand item (shield, cape) must be re-checked, or stowed or pinned, for each new clip family. Run the shield and cape clearance check on every clip the moment it is wired in, not only the combat ones.
- Pieces that follow cloth or physics (the cape) must start hanging cleanly whenever he appears or is set facing another way, be carried by the pose's own moves (only travel may trail them), and be limited in how far they can stand out. Check the first 8 frames of every state and every fast change for floating or ragged leftovers.
- A held weapon's grip is measured on Mixamo's own armed characters (their Paladin, W and Prop files), never inferred from the empty hand; a clip's hit timing and hand path are read from its log (`-SuitLogClips`, JEEBPATH) before its CSV row is written. A row with blank cut points just holds the first frame.
- A run with a held two-handed weapon is judged in a full-body frame from the side and from behind: he stays upright and the arms and chest visibly move.
- Run JEEBSELF on every new look of a clip-driven move, not only the first. Alternate clip rows keep body and head clearance above zero in every frame, and each row's hit window contains its own peak tip speed, not at the window's edge.
- A move that starts after walking into place starts only after its entry condition is true (speed about 0), tapers its stop over about 0.2 s, and never shows one frame of the action pose on the way. Check the worst bone change on the action's first frame: anything laid over the pose after the blend (the two-handed tool grip) needs its own eased weight, or the blend can't soften it.
- When a clip drives a slow gait at an unusual speed, measure the planted-foot slide in the log (the aim shuffle skated at 150 cm/s; his own walk's legs at that speed fall to about 10).
- Moves come from reference key poses (the owner's clips), never guessed.
- Judge walking from frames well into the walk (about 12 to 28), not the first frames.
- Measure first with log lines (JEEBJUMP, JEEBDASH, JEEBSWEEP, JEEBWEIGHT); a wrong number should cost a rebuild, not a review round.
- MoveLab views: B faces +X; R faces +Y (that is where the guarding knight stands). Use `-LabKnight=` and `-LabKnightYaw=` to put the target in shot and in reach.
- Ground friction kills a slide: use separate braking with no friction for a skid.
- `LaunchCharacter` with a zero vector does nothing; use `StopMovementImmediately` to stop someone.
- Test taps just under and just over every input threshold (e.g. `-ShotMove=hop100` / `hop170` around the jump's hold times), not only a quick tap and a long hold.
- Plates at a bending joint (knee, elbow cops): line the plate's middle up with the real joint first, then give it one even set of weights so it stays solid, and check it from the side, front and back at the fully bent frame, not only at rest: off-joint plates lever away from the limb, and one that looks solid from the side can still show stepped seams and a sharp tip from other angles.
- Share only the plate itself between two bones, never part of a neighbouring ring. A forearm's collar half caught in the elbow plate's share turned at two speeds and twisted into claws at a full bend; blunting the points didn't help until the collar followed the forearm alone. (2026-11-05, the arm harness.)
- Check every moving joint at full bend for loose pointed tips: pieces that look clean at rest develop spikes where plates overlap when they turn.
- A clip-driven move plan must give every move's natural clip length against its game timing window (cap the warp at about 1.6x where possible), prove locomotion and foot sliding alone first, and keep the old pose as the fallback for windows too short for a clip. (2026-11-05, plan review of the new suit's animator.)
- Choose clips by measuring them first (`-SuitLogClips`, JEEBPATH: hand, foot and hip through the clip), not from their names: SlashPower turned out to be a 40 cm leap that can't be held at its wind-up, and SlashIn holds both hits of the jab in one clip. A clip's "ahead" can also carry the hips nearly a metre forward; where the capsule does the travelling, take that out (KnightMoves.csv HipLock).
- When a held item sits badly across a set of clips, measure before re-posing: compare the item's axis with the way the hand travels out from the chest over several swings, forehands and backhands together. The new fist was turned about 126 degrees against the hand the clips were made on; no grip tweak could have fixed that.
- Measure on the thing drawn, not a log written earlier in the frame: JEEBSWEEP's tip read the old pose and hid that an added wrist dip was putting the tip through the floor (JEEBANIM `tip` reads the new bones).
- When a landing or any other clip looks stiff, measure the pose itself before re-choosing it. FallLand has a 25 cm knee bend, but the code's "always full body" Speed of 1000 turned its legs off through the `LegsFull` 1 rule: stiff legs from a bug, not the clip. Every airborne state still needs a knee bend and hip dip that grow with the fall. (2026-11-05.)
- Blends out of a move need to be longer than blends into one: a deep lunge or crouch that returns to stance in 0.2 s pops. Use about 0.3-0.36 s out of attacks and keep short ones (0.05-0.12 s) for going in, where snap is wanted. Put the blend's length on the state, not each clip.
- A clip whose end is warped hard (the kick's last third ran 2.5x) looks like a pop whatever the blend: end the clip earlier and let the blend carry the rest.
- A held item's lowest point is checked against the floor in every frame of every move, and clamped in one place (`KeepTipUp`), not fixed in the one clip where someone noticed it. Side pictures taken from above exaggerate how low a blade reaching toward the camera is; read the logged height too.
- A roll is chosen by its hip height through the move: a clip with hips over 40 cm while the body is inverted reads as a dive, not a tumble. Check a reversed clip's end pose in particular: a backward roll made by reversing shouldn't reach the clip's forward lean; fade out at the tuck.
- A get-up starts while he still lies flat (start the clip before the sit-up, not at it), and its first 0.25 s is a blend, or lying to half-sitting pops. Check that the floor clips don't sink his boots (`feet` under about 5 in the log).
- A big whole-body twist in a slash clip turned his back on what he was hitting: `SpineKeep` in KnightMoves.csv leaves part of the chest's turn with the stance. Look for it especially where the legs keep running under the swing.
- Layers fading by a weight (bow, block, crouch) still need a blend when their phase changes, and the layer must keep its last pose while it fades, not swap to another clip (the bow's AimIdle during lowering made a one-frame jump).
- A landing needs a dip at every speed, not only from standing: on the run, the knees give for a moment under the stride, scaled by how fast he came down.
- Size a blend by how far the last pose is from the next, and check it on the sheet instead of trusting the number. A key that can fire mid-move (a rebound) needs its own blend measured.
- A looping clip used as a one-shot reaches its end still at full speed, and it looks like a pop however long the blend after it is: ease the clip's time to a stop (the smash's recovery).
- Check that a test really plays the clip you mean: two test names giving identical numbers means they ran the same thing (`knock` and `drop` both played the sweep's fall until the test pushed him, so the kick's fall had never been seen).
- A knockdown turns him over no more than the blow's force justifies; a flip reads as a ragdoll unless his legs were really swept away.
- Check each walking clip against the stance it blends from: CrouchWalk stands 36 cm taller than CrouchIdle, so he rose the moment he moved. Mixing the idle's legs back in keeps him low.
- Items fixed rigidly to a limb (the shield on the forearm) must be checked at the clip's extremes: rolls turn the shield edge-on and stick it out.
- Performance, for when the animator goes live for every knight: clips and name tables must be one shared copy loaded once, with names turned into indices at load; each knight then keeps only its poses and timers. The hand twist and hip offsets baked at load must run once only. Add update-rate-by-distance, and LODs for the suit.
- Match a drawn line's shape, not only its two ends: compare the tip's height where it crosses his centre and the slope at each end with the drawing, and log the asked-for against the drawn tip through the hit window. A blend still decaying after an override can pull the cut 30 cm off its line: apply the override after the blend too.
- When a move chains from another move's held end, check the foot slide and hip travel across that switch, not only from guard. Keep the previous stance's legs through the follow-up (upper body only) or make the change a visible step; never deliver a blow while the body backs out of the last one's lunge.
- When chained clips stand in different stances, a 60 cm stance change inside an inertial leg blend still skates at 500-600 cm/s (the blend's first frames carry most of the distance): make it a lifted step of about 0.2 s or more, or hold the first move's legs as a fixed pose, and log both directions.
- Compare reach from the peak along the arc (or interpolated), not 30 Hz samples of a 4000 cm/s tip, against the first swing from the same starting stance. If the arm is already straight, `ExtendArm` adds nothing: the extra reach has to come from the body (chest turn, `SpineKeep`, a step or lean).
- Log the tip height through every move's blend back to guard, not only through the move: a held end far from the guard (blade straight up) lets the blend swing the blade down past the guard and back up. End the hold nearer the guard's direction and give the hand the body's out-blend time.
- Shape a drawn cut's height by how far the tip has travelled along the drawn line (its sideways progress), not by time, and check that the clip's sideways travel falls inside the hit window: a time-shaped height on a clip that crosses late becomes a chop and then a flat drift, landing outside the active frames.
- When a move borrows another clip's legs for a gather, pin the planted feet (or pick a clip time with the same stance width), and give every chained switch, both ways (1 into 2 and 2 into 1), the same gather; check each one's foot slide in the log.
- A turn allowed during a move must settle onto its heading, not run at a constant speed and stop dead in a frame; check the frame where it arrives. When a film is meant to judge a body part (the legs in the air, a camera), frame the shot so that part is whole and in the middle, or the check is wasted.
- When the owner asks for more of something (a lean), put the old and new side frames next to each other, the same camera, and hand over both. A film meant to judge a charge must include the charge: check the frame numbers against the log's phases first.
- (Climbing, 2026-11-12) Every one-shot traversal move (grab, ledge climb-up, mantle, vault, let-go) starts from a pose placed at the contact point and blends out over at least 0.2 s into the next state, so no hand or foot is measured inside the surface and nothing pops to standing within a tenth of a second. A jump-to-hang clip used as a grab from the ground pushed the hands into the rock: blend straight into the climbing loop instead.
- (Climbing, round 2) A traversal exit blend must be applied at the state level for every exit path (ledge, vault, block, let-go, landing), and each fix confirmed by re-shooting and diffing the frames; a clip-only fix leaves the pop at the hand-off to locomotion.
- (Climbing, round 3) When a clip passes through a flat or inverted torso (vault, mantle, ledge pull-up), judge it by the pose at every frame, not by the clip's name: cut it before a lean-back phase, and keep the stow-follow and exit-blend flags armed on every key in the family and on every exit path, including a double key change such as the let-go.
- (Climbing, round 4) When a one-shot ends in a stand or a pause, check it lasts long enough to show in the pictures (at least four), not only that the log says it is set.
- (Climbing plan, 2026-11-12) A climbing or mantle plan must state, for every clip, its natural travel per cycle and its warp against the game speed (stay under 1.6x), the hands' and feet's distance to the surface at each wall angle, and the phase handover when the direction changes, and measure them in logs before any film or review.

- (Climbing plan, 2026-11-12) When prediction state lives in a custom movement component (stamina, action timers), send the server's copy back inside the correction data and restore it on the client before the replay, or replays restore the client's own stale history and drift forever; the server reads only inputs from the move, never state.

- (Swimming, 2026-11-14) A server deadline in a predicted mode (drowning, any timer ending in death) must compare the server's own clock against the time the client's moves have covered and drain the difference; "did a move arrive recently" is not enough, since a client can send sparse moves with tiny steps. And a new mode's refusals belong in every server path that starts the action (the bow's ServerBowDraw through StartDraw, DoInv's select, gather and world ops), not only in the client's key handlers.
- (Climbing, round 4) A time or length a scripted move adds to its duration is captured when the move starts and travels in the correction data with it; re-reading a live tuning value every frame against a stored duration lets two machines disagree on the path.
- When a client-side reach or range changes, change the server's matching check with it, with slack for lag. Better, share one constant instead of keeping two literals.
- A state that now lasts many seconds (a repeating gather) must be ended by every event that should end it (death, stun, knockdown, the tool leaving his hand); a single movement check does not cover them.

(2026-11-02, the jump squat.)
- A prediction rule that runs on replayed moves may only read what the saved move restores for the START of that move (bPressedJump, JumpKeyHoldTime); derived counters like JumpCurrentCount are one move behind in a replay.

- Test-only code lives inside `-JeebShot` (`bShotMode`) branches.
- Servers and players must run the same Tuning.ini and CSVs: repackage after changing movement numbers.

## Code pitfalls
- `FMath::SmoothStep(A, B, X)` needs A < B; use `JeebWorld::Smooth` for ramps that go down.
- Don't call `GetGravityZ` in a constructor (use 980 x GravityScale).
- PowerShell: matching text in CRLF files needs `` `r`n ``, and short function names can clash with aliases (R, Rp); prefer the Edit tool for multi-line changes.

- Text on any browse board (titles, headers, row labels) needs its own clear strip outside the item area and must be legible from where the player first sees it; check from the start spot as well as close up.
- Judge slot cuts first on the flat-colour per-slot render (ragged cuts show as other-slot fringes at hems), and give reviewers crops where the knight fills at least half the frame.
- When a slot's cut moves, check every OTHER set under it too (a helmet cut raised exposed a neck the mail's hidden hood used to cover): picture each style with the neighbouring piece off.
- When tilting or rotating a worn piece, pivot it about where it meets its neighbour (the helmet's collar rim), not its own centre, then re-check every seam (back and sides): the far edge swings into or away from the neighbour. (2026-11-11, helmet tilt 8 degrees.)
- Judge a new tier colour in the game at the tier's own close-up, next to its neighbours in the same light: a hue shift that separates two shiny tiers can overshoot into a saturated paint look; keep some white in it.

## Far trees and stand-ins (2026-11-13, far tree crowns)
- LESSON: a cheap far stand-in for culled geometry must match the near asset's average colour and light response, measured against the same background at the hand-over distance at noon AND the low sun (a stand-in darker or flatter than the real thing reads as a flicker or a hole while the dither fades it in), and must cost less in pixels and triangles than what it replaces (no shadows, small meshes, one material, folded away while the near asset still shows).
- A solid shell standing in for airy cards (a cone for needle boughs) needs a higher shade floor and a calmer cut-out outline than a lumpy puff: with no bright gaps it reads near black against a low sun, and a strong rim cut-out tears it into shards. Judge it from the ground looking toward the sun, not only from the air.
- UE 5.8's Python has no `MaterialExpressionDitherTemporalAA` and no `MaterialExpressionShadowPassSwitch`: cut-outs are plain, and a shadow-only branch goes in a Custom node as `#if SHADOW_DEPTH_SHADER`. A class missing from Python must be checked for None before `g.node`, or the material build crashes.

## Hero trees (2026-11-13, the plaza oak)
- LESSON: judge a big canopy by the shade it casts and by the unlit side of its bark, not only by the sunlit crown. The ground beneath it and the trunk must keep readable colour at noon and at the low sun. Its colour balance must match the neighbouring trees, and its average colour must come from the tint ramp, not the painted highlights: a Meshy leaf texture's bright tips multiply a gold tint up to pale yellow.
- A real crown is several leaf shells deep, so cutting gaps in each shell still adds up to a solid shadow. A lighter, dappled shade needs a hidden shadow copy (bCastHiddenShadow) with shafts cut through the whole crown plus fine thinning. Judge the shade from eye level, where players walk, as well as from above: a shaded lawn seen at a grazing angle can read navy-black even when it is green from above.

## Ground (2026-11-13, world ground, roads and foundations)
- LESSON: a ground material shared by meshes of different resolution takes its masks from one function at shared vertices, so seams and wear match; test every ground picture at noon and the low sun, from above and at eye level, for hay tint, tiling and straight edges.
- LESSON: every soft edge in a ground blend uses centred noise ((n - 0.5) times a weight) and an offset that keeps it at zero where its mask is zero: an uncentred noise term painted the 1.7 m noise picture across every field as a grid of dirt dots. Fade every tiled picture fully to its average far away, and break a repeat with a second sampling at an unrelated size.
- LESSON: ground detail (worn patches, dirt, rock, stones) is judged at eye level and from about 8 m and 30 m up; dirt has soft, low-contrast edges and sits where feet wear it (doors, junctions, road edges), and anything smaller than the grass around it (stones under 30-40 cm meadow) needs the grass thinned or shortened there or it vanishes. Masks finer than a grass cell's sample grid (1-2 m aprons on a 1.9 m grid) must be checked per tuft. The near/far grass hand-over is checked for matching hue and variation, not excused as an expected ring.
- LESSON: when a colour fix goes into one material layer, list every other layer that draws the same kind of thing (road dirt, wall-base soil, the far-distance fade colour of rock) and check each in the pictures; a distant stone's colour is its fade average, judged against green hills through the haze.
- LESSON (road edges, 2026-11-13): a road-edge mask needs two unrelated noise scales plus a width that drifts along the road, and is judged in a close crop for duplicated lobes and a crisp contour, not only for how far the dirt spreads.
- LESSON (road edges, 2026-11-14): a worn street edge is a wide soft band in which whole stones drop out one by one (a per-stone number against the band) with earth washed over the last ones, never a threshold through the stone picture; check it in a close crop at the shipped 60% render scale for stair-stepped contours, pixel crumbs and near-black fringes in shade.
- LESSON: judge bare ground and soil in shadow as well as in sun; anything dark in shade next to sunlit grass reads as a stain, so keep low cover (short, sparse tufts) on worn ground by walls rather than clearing it, or give bare earth a brightness floor.
- LESSON (castle island, 2026-11-14): judge the ground material on a patch with no grass on it, close (under 15 m) and from the air: the meadow hides the ground under it, so M_Ground's near grass was black for a day unnoticed. ## Buildings (2026-11-13, collision, window lights, cracks, baked-in pieces)
- LESSON (cracks): weld a Meshy GLB before decimating it (`Tools/building_reduce.py` does): Meshy splits every vertex at its UV seams, and an un-welded collapse pulls the two sides of each seam apart, leaving hairline cracks that show the grass behind as green slivers. Count open edges after reducing (a sound building has a handful).
- LESSON (collision): a building's collision is its own reduced mesh (`SM_<Name>_Col`, complex as simple), placed with exactly the visual mesh's location, turn and scale (non-uniform ScaleZ too), on every machine; a box over the footprint blocks porches, steps and awnings and reads as an invisible wall. - LESSON (baked-in pieces): a barrel, pot or lantern Meshy fused into a wall has no wall behind it; cutting it off leaves a hole no fill closes well. - LESSON (removing props from a baked model, 2026-11-14): pressing or cutting a fused prop and re-texturing the patch from nearby surface still left ghost outlines, spiky rims, flat slabs and smeared mixed materials at 13 of 14 spots on the storybook cottage. - LESSON (lit windows): Meshy paints lit panes yellow in the colour picture too, so dimming only the emissive leaves "lit" windows by day; darken the glass in the colour wherever the glow picture marks a window, and switch each window by its own threshold.

## Water (2026-11-13, Single Layer Water on the sea, rivers, pond and moat)
- LESSON: judge every water (or other lit see-through surface) change at the low golden sun as well as at noon: the water's own scattering is lit by the sun, so at the orange low sun it turns rivers into a muddy floor striped with hard tree shadows. Keep scattering low and fade most of it with `ADayNight::Dusk` (WaterLook.cpp), so the water goes dark and shows the sky's reflection, but never to black: keep a visible slate floor and check the darkest view (looking steeply down, where nothing reflects), not just the pretty one.
- In Single Layer Water, Opacity is foam: even 0.05 shows as pale hard-edged patches. Multiply any foam term by its own mask so it is exactly 0 away from the shore, and keep river shore foam faint and broken (a continuous bright line reads as a white outline; foam is a soft, greyish, see-through edge, never an opaque white shape).
- LESSON: when the owner gives a reference picture, sample the rendered pixels and compare the numbers (hue, saturation, value) at noon and at the low sun; darker water also reads more saturated, so "darker" is not "greyer". Judge ripples with the camera close and looking down, where the reflection fills the frame and blocky normals show.
- LESSON: judge low-sun water through its shadowed and wading views, not only the open reflective ones. Sun-lit scattering paints every tree and wire shadow onto the water as a hard dark stripe; the water can't opt out of receiving shadows, so cut the sun-lit share hard at the low sun (DuskScatter about 0.1) and carry the body colour with an even, unlit term (the emissive `Glow`, slate-teal, not grey-brown) so the shadow contrast drops. Specular sky blobs and foam must never be the brightest thing on the water: soften and cap them.
- Anything that looks wrong inside a dug-out hollow may be the Field map's UE template grid floor (Floor_0 at Z -0.5, MI_ProcGrid), hidden under the land everywhere else: line-trace the spot before blaming the new material.
- LESSON (lineup labels, walking height): check floating labels from walking height, not only an overview, and place row titles above the tallest piece of their row (a copy for the far side too) so nothing stands in front of them at a low angle.
- LESSON (held-item close-ups): the jab films render at 1280x720 whatever -ResX says; for a grip check use a free camera (-ShotEye with -ShotFov about 45) 40-60 cm from the fist, at rest and mid-swing, from back, right and front, instead of cropping a full-body frame.
- LESSON (patches): when a patch replaces a removed object, texture it by sampling the neighbouring surface's UVs (several spots, not one flat colour), and check every close-up for see-through slivers where the cut meets the old geometry; a building added through the pipeline is only finished once the visual reviewer has passed close-ups of every spot a piece was removed from.
