# How to test Jeebsland

**Rule of thumb: the editor's Play button never needs the worlds. The .bat files are the real thing.**

| I want to... | Do this |
|---|---|
| Quickly try a change, alone | Editor: green Play ▶ (any mode). Net Mode "Play Offline". |
| Try it online, quickly (2 players, no setup) | Editor: ⋮ next to Play > Net Mode "Play As Client", Number of Players 2 > ▶. Logs in as Tester 1 / Tester 2. |
| The full game: starting screen, accounts, both worlds | Close the editor. `Tools\HostWorlds.bat`, wait for "Jeebsland world up", then `Tools\RunClient.bat` (twice for 2 players). Address `127.0.0.1`. Account names use only letters, numbers and spaces. |
| See exactly what friends get | Same as above, but open `Packaged\Windows\Jeebsland.exe` instead of RunClient (only right after repackaging). |

In the editor's preview window (New Editor Window / Selected Viewport), Escape stops Play and it can't go full screen: use **O**
for the map / settings screen there. For full screen with a working Escape, pick **Standalone Game** (⋮ next to Play > Modes):
it plays offline straight away. To change the editor's Stop key: Editor
Preferences > Keyboard Shortcuts > search "Stop" > set it to Shift+Escape.

## Oak Hill (2026-11-11)

Field map, Play. Oak Hill is about 185 m east of the start, across the river (there is no bridge yet, so wade across where its west road stops at the bank). The world map (O) shows its name and its streets. Head for them and look for the big oak on the plaza.
- What's there: the plaza with its big oak and the well, the smith, bank, store and potion shop round the ring road, the town hall to the east, the church on its rise to the northeast, and 17 houses (two designs). The streets follow the approved plan. The ground has its own gentle hills: the plaza is just over 6 m above the river and the castle knoll about 9 m. There's grass and there are trees.
- Left empty on purpose, for what isn't made yet: the boxing ring (south of the plaza, levelled), the castle ruins (on the knoll to the north), the garden behind the town hall, the graveyard (southeast), both forts and the watchtower by the river, and the bridge.
- Test pictures from the air: `-ShotEye=X_Y_Z` puts a free camera there, looking along `-ShotYaw` and `-ShotPitch` (`-ShotFov` too). Add `-ShotAt=X,Y` to stand the knight there, so the grass grows round him. `-NoOakHill` (test pictures only) leaves the town out, to compare frame rates; `-NoOakGrass`, `-NoOakMeshes` (also the stones and shrubs at its walls) and `-NoOakTrees` leave out just that part, and `-NoWorldGrass` the meadow grass outside the start's circle and the town (it grows over all the world's grassy ground since 2026-11-12). The plaza is at X -3550, Y 18290; a view of the whole town from the air: `-ShotEye=-9550_13290_6500 -ShotYaw=40 -ShotPitch=-37`. The log's `JEEBOAK` lines give the heights, each road's steepest grade, where each building went, and the closest two buildings.

## Chat and the radar potion (2026-10-30)

- **Chat:** press **Enter**, type, Enter again to send (Escape cancels). Players within 35 m hear it and see it over your head. `/w message` tells the whole world. `/mute Name`, `/unmute Name`, `/mutelist` hide players on your machine. Bad words turn into asterisks (the server does it). Try it with "Play As Client", 2 players.
- **Owner tools:** make `Saved/Moderation.txt` on the server's machine with lines like `mute Sir Jeeb` (they can play but nobody hears them) or `ban Sir Jeeb` (cannot log in). It is re-read every few seconds, no restart. Lines starting with `#` are comments.
- **Radar potion:** the practice pack has three (the store sells it for 60 ingots). Put it in the potion slot (drag it there) and press Q: enemy knights and other players near you show as red dots on the minimap for 2 minutes. Ready again after 3 minutes.

It is out in about 0.11 s and hits like a smash attack charged 20% (damage, hitstun and push), with a smash attack's end lag; on a raised shield it bounces off like a smash. Rooted until it is over.

**Bow (slot 2 of the starting kit; with 99 iron and 99 fire arrows):** with the bow in hand the shield goes away and a quiver of arrows shows on his back. Hold the **left or right** button to draw: he turns side-on, the bow up in his left hand and the string pulled back toward his face, walking at half speed while the camera closes in over his right shoulder and a reticle shows where the arrow will go (the ring closes as the draw fills and turns gold when full). The crossbow works like a sniper rifle: hold the right button to bring it up with the camera in close, and click the left button to shoot (no draw to wait for). Fire arrows glow, light up what is near them, and keep burning what they hit for a few seconds (3 x 2 damage).

## Look board (2026-11-15)

One command shows the whole look at a glance, always from the same 12 views (noon and low sun each) beside the reference pictures:

    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File Tools\LookBoard.ps1

It takes a machine run slot, makes one off-screen Unreal run (about 2 minutes) and writes `Saved\LookBoard\<yyyy-MM-dd_HHmm>.jpg` (a contact sheet, about 0.7 MB; the last 10 are kept). After a change, run it again and then `... LookBoard.ps1 -Compare`: `<time>_compare.jpg` puts the newest two boards side by side, view by view (before | after). `-ProjectDir <folder>` runs it in another copy; `-KeepShots` keeps the raw stills. The views are the `$Views` list at the top of the script (name, X, Y, eye height, yaw, pitch, mask); edit a position there.

## The LOOK tab (2026-11-15; editor Play and test runs only, never in a package)

Press **O** (or Escape in a standalone game), click **LOOK**. The world keeps running behind it, so every slider shows at once. Pick a group along the top (PICTURE, SKY + HAZE, CLOUDS, WATER, GRASS, GROUND). Each slider shows its name, its value (orange once changed), a blue tick where it started, and a **reset** button. The grass reach and density sliders apply when you let go (they regrow the grass). Presets are on the right: click the name box and type, **SAVE AS** (an empty name makes "Look 1", "Look 2"...), click a preset to load it, **x** deletes it. **DEFAULTS** writes `Saved/LookPresets/<name>_defaults.txt`: each changed setting with its old and new default, for making a look permanent. Presets are plain `cvar=value` files in `Saved/LookPresets/`. "Started" means the values when you first opened the tab in that run. Test run: `-ShotLookPanel` (log JEEBLOOK).

**Ground under the grass at dusk (2026-11-15):** in shade and at the low sun the painted ground between the blades no longer glows brighter than the blades. The low-sun fill (`jeeb.Ground.SkyFill` 0.2, GROUND slider "low-sun light past the grass") is a light that shadows cannot dim, so it now only lights the ground past the grass's reach (from half of `jeeb.Grass.PackFade` out); among the blades the ground is the meadow's floor, a touch darker at the low sun (`jeeb.Ground.Floor` 0.9; 1 = same as noon). `jeeb.Ground.UnderFill 1` brings the old glowing look back to compare. Oak Hill's lawn and the world's ground now follow the same setting (`jeeb.TownLab.GroundFill` is gone), so moving the slider no longer leaves a seam at the town's edge. Noon is unchanged.

Tin and Iron are unchanged. The quiver, flask and resting bow are console variables too: `jeeb.Knight.Quiver X_Y_Z_Pitch_Yaw_Roll` (-16_21_-29_-5_0_4: snug on the right hip), `jeeb.Knight.Flask X_Y_Z_Pitch_Yaw_Roll_Scale` (-16.5_-16.5_14_13_0_172_0.8: leaning in), `jeeb.Knight.BowRestRoll` -110 (degrees counterclockwise seen from behind: the string side toward his body, a little below level). ## Tree groves (2026-11-15; TEMPORARY, editor Play and test builds only)

Field map, Play (you start at Oak Hill's plaza). Walk straight east out of the town, past the church, about 130 m from the plaza oak to open meadow: five small groves in a row running north-south, each about 23 m across with a plank sign on its west side (they are at X -9400 to 5400, Y 31000; `-ShotAt=-2000,29000` stands you there in a test picture). From the south end to the north end: **Spruce Forest** (Project Nature: 3 full, 4 half-grown and 3 young spruces, 8 saplings between), **DZ Sample Trees** (aspen, cork oak, pine and the two palms; aspens and cork oaks in autumn colours), **English Oak** and **European Hornbeam** (Megaplants; their leaves stay summer green, the fallen leaves under them are autumn), and **Our Own Trees** (what the woods plant today: oaks and pines). The real grass and the fallen leaves grow round and under them. Open World Foliage has no trees or bushes (only grass and flowers), so it has no grove. Nothing in them is solid. To leave them out: console `jeeb.Test.Groves 0` before the next Play (or `jeeb.Test.Groves=0` under `[SystemSettings]` in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`); `ProjectNotes` "Tree groves" says how to delete them for good. Log: `JEEBGROVE`.
