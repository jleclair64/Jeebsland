# Jeebsland project notes (the full history)

Moved out of CLAUDE.md on 2026-11-15 so every session reads less. CLAUDE.md keeps the standing rules and the current state; this file keeps the detail of every system and batch. Search it (Grep) for the system you are working on before changing it.


Unreal Engine 5.8 C++ project (Windows). Game code lives in `Source/Jeebsland/`. Most of the world (forest, village, castle, pond, etc.) is built procedurally in C++ actors rather than placed assets.

- Default game mode: `KnightGameMode` (set in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`); the world is built by `KnightGameState`. Map: `/Game/Field`.
- The UE template code and content (Variant_*, Characters, ThirdPerson, LevelPrototyping, template game mode / controller) were deleted 2026-10-12. `AJeebslandCharacter` stays: it is the knight's base class (camera boom and camera). StateTree plugins are off.
- `Game Updates.txt` is the owner's private notes. Don't read or act on it; requests come through chat.

## Art direction (owner, 2026-11-05; applies to every design choice)

This is permanent and goes into every design from now on.

- **World, weapons and items:** a Breath of the Wild meets Overwatch look. Simple, but smooth and detailed. Focus on good colours and textures, not realism, but still pretty and natural. Think of how the forests of Fortnite look, the medieval towns in Overwatch and the landscape of Breath of the Wild.
- **Items** (weapons, tools, potions and so on): a mash-up of Breath of the Wild, Overwatch, Fortnite, Skyrim and RuneScape, slightly cartoonish, an HD RuneScape feel. Good detail, smooth, not too small. In practice (owner, same day, after seeing both ends and sending reference pictures): "not quite realistic but a tiny bit cartoonish". Sleek, natural proportions only a little bolder than real life, crisp bevels, hand-painted textures, rich clean colours, polished wood and dark steel, no rust or dirt. Too realistic was the first thin, weathered set; too cartoonish was fat, toy-like, banana-shaped forms. Wood is rich brown with just a touch of painted grain and a little natural variance: not yellow or orange, no drastic colour change along it. **`Docs/ItemStyleGuide.md` is the recipe (the process, Meshy settings, prompt wording, proportions, and the approved pictures in `Docs/ItemStyle/`): read it before designing any item, so the balance between cartoonish and realistic never has to be found again.**
- **Armour** is the exception: it may lean more towards realism.
- **Grass** is the second exception (owner, 2026-11-15: "I'd like to lean towards realism with the grass"): natural, photo-real blades rather than painted strokes. Candidate: Project Nature's free "temperate Vegetation" grass library and meadow flowers on Fab (each blade its own mesh); see `Docs/TownLab/AssetScout.md`.

Say this in every Meshy prompt and check every new model, material and building against it.

## Model and effort

The owner wants a recommendation on model and effort for each request, to balance quality against token use. Before executing any large task (or a batch of tasks), always suggest the most optimal model and effort first. "Optimal" means balancing token usage while still getting a great result that won't need rework. For a mixed batch, say which items suit which model. For small requests, one line is enough, and only if it differs from the current setting. Don't switch unless asked.
- Sonnet 5.5, low or medium effort: tweaks to values, colors, sizes or positions; removing things; small single-file changes.
- Sonnet 5.5, high effort: moderate features or multi-file changes along existing patterns.
- Opus 5.5, high effort: new systems (skills UI, enemies, combat, dungeons), tricky visual or rendering bugs, anything Sonnet failed at.

## Ending a session

When the owner says goodbye, goodnight or anything similar, don't just sign off: first ask if they'd like you to commit and refresh the "Current state" notes below for their next chat. If they say yes, update that section (what was just done, what's unfinished, known issues, decisions, anything they want carried over), remove stale lines, keep it to about a screen, and commit. In the same question, also ask whether to repackage the game (only if code or content changed since the last package; then make `Packaged/Jeebsland.zip` without the `.pdb`, and remind them to restart the worlds with `Tools/HostWorlds.bat` and send friends the new zip).

## Animation direction (owner, 2026-11-05; replaces the 2026-10-30 rule)

The knight may use animation clips as well as code-driven poses; the old "keep the knight code-driven" rule is scratched. The goal is natural movement, with moves that fit together and never look choppy as they switch. References: Breath of the Wild, Skyrim, Super Smash Bros, and other games.

How the suit's animator works (`FKnightAnimator`, 2026-11-05):
- Code writes the bones of a `UPoseableMeshComponent`; clips are read at load as dense frames x bones from `Content/Data/KnightClips.bin` (list `ClipNames`). Game state chooses a key (`ChooseAction`; the old rules, timers and hit windows still decide everything) and each key change starts an inertial blend (a decaying offset from the old pose, so no pop and no cross-fade), with the right hand's rate capped so the blade doesn't whip.
- Layers: locomotion (9 clips by direction and speed on a shared, distance-based phase), overlays from the waist (crouch, block, bow), then actions (one-shots, time-warped so their hit and follow-through land on the game's own hit windows).
- `Content/Data/KnightMoves.csv` holds each move's clip, cut points (a tool swing's ClipHit is the top of the wind-up and ClipFollow the blow; sit and slide play to ClipHit and hold), legs mode (`LegsFull` 2 full, 1 by speed, 0 upper only), blend time, `HipLock` (removes a clip's own forward hip travel where the capsule does the moving) and `SpineKeep` (how much of the chest's turn stays with the stance, more while running, so a slash doesn't turn his back on the target). No build needed; for falls `ClipHit` is seconds shown, for jump, land and hits it is playback rate.
- `PlaceGear` moves `WristR`, `ElbowL` and `UpperPivot` onto the clip's bones so the weapon, trail and hit boxes follow it. `SolveGear` measures how the weapon, shield, bow and quiver sit on the bones, once, at load.
- Last step each frame, after the blend: the hips rise if a boot would sink (`Sole`), and `KeepTipUp` turns the wrist to keep the sword's tip at least 5 cm off the floor.
- After a fight he stands on guard (`Idle_SS`, 4 s, `CombatT`) so sword clips start and end in the same stance, then eases back to `Rest`.
- Adding a clip: download FBX from Mixamo (on its stock Y Bot) into `Art/Knight Chainmail/Clips`, run `Tools/ImportKnightClips.ps1` with the editor closed (`knight_clip_retarget.py` in Blender, `knight_clip_import.py` in Unreal), add the name to `ClipNames` and a CSV row; the editor rebakes `KnightClips.bin` at its next run, so commit that file. `knight_suit_import.py` wipes `/Game/NewSuit`, so re-import the clips and the bare knight after any suit import.
- Test pictures: `-ShotMove=...`, `-SuitWalk=<dir>_<share>_<from>_<for>`, `-SuitSprint`, `-SuitDo=roll<deg>|stun<hundredths>|knock|drop|crouch`, `-SuitClip=` / `-SuitClipAt=`, `-SuitGallery`, `-SuitLogClips`, `-LabNoDummies`, `-ShotSeconds=N`. ## Climbing and the mantle (2026-11-12; Opus agent, not yet seen by the owner)

- Free climbing like Breath of the Wild and a Fortnite-style mantle are custom movement modes in the knight's own movement component, `UKnightMovement` (KnightMovement.h/.cpp; set on the knight through `AJeebslandCharacter`/`AKnightCharacter`'s FObjectInitializer constructors). They run inside Unreal's movement prediction: the player's machine predicts, the server runs the same moves from the same inputs (the keys' direction, Space, Ctrl) and corrects position, mode, stamina and the scripted move (`FKnightMoveResponse` carries the climbing state in corrections). Never the player's word for where he is. Modes (`JeebClimb::EMode`): 1 on the rock, 2 the climb jump, 3 the ledge climb-up, 4 mantle over, 5 mantle onto. - What can be climbed (`Climbable`): a component tagged `JeebClimb` (the world's ground in WorldTerrain.cpp, the original square in GrassField.cpp, MoveLab's rock), only where too steep to walk and at most 15 degrees overhanging. Never buildings, trees, fences, walls, gear, mining rocks or Oak Hill's ground (untagged). Grabbing: walk or jump into it until he touches it (within 50 degrees of square on; the probes run only after a move that touched tagged rock, `HandleImpact`, so a busy server pays nothing for everyone else), not holding Ctrl, not right after letting go (until he lands), not with an emptied bar.
- Mantle (general rule since 2026-11-16, owner: "any object just too high for the knight to jump over, but not so high that it looks weird ... stumps and crates and other medium sized objects he can't walk through"): any solid whose top is `MantleMin` 60 .. `MantleMax` 145 cm above his feet, judged from its own collision by `UKnightMovement::MantleCandidate`, no tags needed: it blocks pawns, isn't a pawn or a replicated actor (creatures, foes, loot: only the world every machine builds for itself from the same seeds, so server and client agree), isn't simulating physics, isn't tagged `JeebClimb` (rock climbs instead) or `JeebNoMantle` (component or actor: the opt-out), its own box (for an instanced set, that instance's box: the fill's solids are thousands of boxes in one component) is at least `MantleMinWidth` 40 cm long (no posts or poles) and under 25 m, its top no more than MantleMax + 30 over his feet (buildings, trees, palisades, lamp posts fail here), and the side he meets stands on his ground (not a beam or a deck). `JeebMantle` still forces one in (MoveLab's boards). No traces in that check, so `HandleImpact` runs it on every bump and only then does `PlanMantle` trace: the first thing at knee, waist or chest height ahead must be a candidate and square on (a side, not a slope), the top just past its face flat and a candidate too (a crate on a crate: the stack's top), nothing above; then samples along the top every 15 cm find how deep it runs: deep enough to stand on (2R+10) he climbs onto it, else he vaults to the ground just past its far side (never closer than the old fixed 2R+30, so the vault keeps its stride; a landing nearly as high as the top or with no room is refused). Space by one, walking or running square into one (`MantleAuto` 1, owner 2026-11-13; 0 = Space only), or running into one in the air. The pose scales the vault's and climb-onto's own hip rise by the real height over the heights they were approved at (1 m fence, 1.2 m block; `WallPlay`). ## Parallel agents for every multi-part request (owner, 2026-11-11; standing rule, never wait to be reminded)

When a request has several independent parts, split them across background agents from the START of the turn, scaled to the size (a quick fix needs none; Opus for motion, visuals and new systems, Sonnet for data and colour tweaks). Usage limit (owner, 2026-11-13, said twice): whenever background agents run, start a CronCreate guard every 5 minutes that reads `mcp__ccd_session_mgmt__get_usage`; at about 97% of the 5-hour limit, have every agent finish its command, release its Unreal lock and stop, write a resume note, and schedule a one-shot 2 minutes after the reset that resumes each agent with SendMessage. Cron jobs die with the session: set the guard again each session.

Each agent works in its own project copy (owner, 2026-11-13, "let's do it"). This is the default for EVERY agent that builds or runs Unreal, from its first step: make the copies before briefing the agents, never "from the next batch". (2026-11-14 the switch was put off to the next batch while seven agents queued for hours in the main folder; the owner had to ask "I thought we said we were going to run multiple copies at the same time".) If agents are already working in the main folder when this comes up, move them at once: each stops at a point that compiles and releases its lock, one local checkpoint commit (not pushed), `AgentCopy.sh new` for each agent with real work left, and a brief with its copy's path. Agents only minutes from done may stay. Any process change the owner approves applies to the work running now, not just to later work.
- `bash Tools/AgentCopy.sh new <agent>` makes `../JeebsAgents/<agent>` on branch `agent/<agent>` from main's latest commit, with the big git-ignored assets linked in (Content/Filler, Art originals); `remove <agent>` unlinks them first, then deletes the copy (only once merged). Commit everything in the main folder before making copies, or the copies start without it.
- The agent edits, builds (Build.bat with the copy's .uproject) and runs test pictures only in its copy, and commits to its own branch (never pushes). Another agent's half-finished code can never break its build.
- `Tools/uqueue.sh` shares the machine: at most 3 Unreal processes at once (6 cores, 32 GB, 8 GB GPU; a fourth only slows the others), first come first served, one build at a time per copy, `solo` for frame-rate timing (holds every slot). Same rules as below: run it in the background, release the moment Unreal exits, `-RenderOffScreen`, `-abslog=<copy>/Saved/Logs/<agent>.log`.
- A copy's first build compiles the whole game module (about 10-20 minutes, ~5 GB); shaders come from the shared engine cache. Disk: about 8 GB a copy (127 GB free on 2026-11-14). Keep copies between jobs and `reset` them after merging, so their builds stay warm. `remove` only when the disk runs short.
- The main session merges the branches into main, settles conflicts, runs the shared rebuilds once, checks the whole, commits and pushes. Unreal assets (.uasset, .umap) can't be merged: each asset folder has one owning agent per batch.
- Linked shared assets belong to the main folder: a copy rebuilds them only if its brief says so.

How agents shared Unreal in one folder (owner, 2026-11-13: "find a smarter/optimal way"; a free-for-all with one lock had five agents waiting while one held it 75 minutes; still the rule for work in the main folder):
- `Tools/ulock.sh` is a first-come-first-served queue. `build NAME` holds the exclusive build lock (`Saved/unreal.lock`) for builds, importers, material builds, the editor and packaging. `run NAME` holds one of two run slots for `-game` / test-picture runs, so two can go at once but never during a build. `release NAME` gives it back; `status` shows the queue. Waiting blocks, so agents run it in the background. Stale locks clear themselves when no Unreal process has run for 3 minutes; holders past 30 min (build) or 12 min (run) show OVERDUE.
- Every test run: `-RenderOffScreen -Multiprocess -LiveCoding=false` (Live Coding is also off in `Config/DefaultEditorPerProjectUserSettings.ini`: a running Live Coding console blocks every copy's build; no window over the owner's games; `-Multiprocess` skips the startup SDK check, which otherwise sits idle until any build on the machine ends, and stops runs writing shared config files) and `-abslog=<repo>/Saved/Logs/<agent>.log` (two runs at once would share Jeebsland.log). GPU timing is measured alone.
- Never edit `Tools/uqueue.sh` or `ulock.sh` in place while agents are waiting in them (bash reads a running script as it goes, so the waiting copies break): write a new file and `mv` it over the old one.
- Launching Unreal from Git Bash: give it Windows paths (`cygpath -w`; a `/c/Users/...` path reaches it as `c/Users/...` and pops a visible "Failed to open descriptor file" dialog, 2026-11-14) and put `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` before the command (or launch from PowerShell). Otherwise bash rewrites the map `/Game/Field` into `C:/Program Files/Git/Game/Field` and the run hangs with no level (2026-11-14: one run sat 36 minutes holding every slot). Give every run a time limit, and kill it if it overruns.
- Hold only while Unreal runs: never while thinking, editing or waiting for reviewers.
- Batch per agent: finish every edit first (a build compiles everyone's code, so never leave a half-edit in a file), one build, then all pictures in as few runs as possible.
- A build failing in another agent's file: release and tell the coordinator at once with the error lines.
- Size the batch: at most about four agents that need Unreal at once; Blender, data, research and Meshy/Mixamo work can run beside them freely.

## How much checking a change gets (owner, 2026-11-15: "make sure we're not doing an unnecessary amount of checks"; overrides "every change" below)

- **Small** (numbers, colours the owner named, removals, one-file tweaks): no pictures, no reviewers. Build, and the owner looks in the editor.
- **Medium** (tuning a look, moving or resizing things): build, one quick look of my own (a few pictures or log lines), then the owner judges it live. A reviewer only where something could clip, break, cost frame time or go wrong online.
- **Large** (new systems, combat, online, new models or materials in the world, anything the owner can't easily judge by eye): the full reviewer rounds below.

- **At most two review rounds.** After two FAILs, stop and bring the owner the pictures and the problem; change the approach, not just the values.
- **Timing only when frame time can change,** batched into one solo window. Pictures and other work run in the free slots meanwhile; nothing sits idle waiting for solo.

## Time of day

`ADayNight` (DayNight.h): the sun circles once every 20 minutes (`HalfLoopSeconds` 600, the server's clock), 41 degrees up at noon (phase 0) and 25 below at midnight (phase 0.5): elevation = 8 + 33 cos(2 pi phase), so the loop grew rather than shifted (2026-11-17; until then 26 + 15 cos, a midnight sun that never set). It sets at phase 0.289 and rises at 0.711: about 11.6 min of day, 8.4 of night. `ADayNight::Dusk` (1 - smoothstep 13..24 degrees) is still 0..1 and now stays 1 all night (lamps, lit windows, grass and water dusk tints); `ADayNight::Night` (1 - smoothstep -10..2) drives the night look and the stars.

**Night lighting (2026-11-17).** Stylised, readable nights (Zelda, Sea of Thieves, Fortnite) rather than real darkness. The handover: the sun's light on the ground (its diffuse and specular scale, in 1/20 steps since each change rebuilds its render state) fades from 4 degrees up to 1 down, and its shadows stop there; the sun stays the sky atmosphere's light 0 for the twilight glow until 16 down, then is hidden (no light pass). The **moonlight** is a second directional light made by `ADayNight::TickMoon`: sky atmosphere light index 1 (lights the night sky and clouds faintly; disc hidden, the moon is `AMoon`), from `MoonDir` (30 degrees up, azimuth 205; was 15), fading in from 1 to 8 degrees down and casting the shadows from 1 down (the same reach and cascades as the sun's, no far cascade), so there is never more than one shadow caster; hidden by day (no cost). Forward shading priority 2 (water, glass, fog take it at night). The armour glint follows it at night. On top: the sky light times `jeeb.Night.Ambient`, exposure plus `jeeb.Night.Exposure` stops, a blue paler grade (`jeeb.Night.Tint`: ColorGain, less saturation, no warm highlights), a blue lift in the shadows (`jeeb.Night.ShadowLift`), the haze a dim blue fixed on screen (divided by the exposure), the sun's haze glow off, the LOW cloud ring and NightSky's painted clouds dimmed and moonlit, trails a moonlit blue-grey. Live settings (LOOK tab, SKY + HAZE, "Night:" sliders): `jeeb.Night.Moon` 0.5, `jeeb.Night.MoonColor` "0.60 0.72 1.00", `jeeb.Night.MoonShadows` 1, `jeeb.Night.Ambient` 2, `jeeb.Night.Exposure` 0.5, `jeeb.Night.Tint` 0.5 (eased in with Night squared, so the golden hour keeps its colour), `jeeb.Night.ShadowLift` 0.012, `jeeb.Night.Sky` 0.5 (the sky atmosphere's SkyLuminanceFactor at night: a deep blue where the stars show), `jeeb.Night.Twilight` 0.6 (extra stops round the horizon, none 7 degrees either side: the handover never dips), `jeeb.Sun.Low` -25. Ramps: sun on the ground smoothstep -1..4 degrees, moon 1 - smoothstep -8..-1, Night 1 - smoothstep -6..8, the sun as sky light until -16. Phases for pictures: 0 noon, 0.2645 sunset glow (5 up), 0.289 sunset, 0.3417 10 down, 0.5 midnight, 0.72 dawn (2 up).

**Sunset haze (2026-11-17, owner: "why does the distant horizon glow grey at sunset?").** The height fog's inscattering colour is a self-lit luminance: it stayed at full strength through sunset while the exposure lifts about 2.5 stops there (LowLift 1.56 + Twilight 0.6 + part of Night.Exposure), so far hills and forest showed a flat haze about 6x brighter than noon's, tone-mapped to grey-white over a dark land. `ADayNight::TickLook` now scales the day haze by the light on the land as a share of noon's (the sun's on the ground, SunGround x sin(elevation), plus a sky term fading from 10 degrees up to 8 down), blended by `jeeb.Look.HazeFollow` (1; 0 the old fixed glow), and tints it toward the low sun's colour by `jeeb.Look.HazeSunTint` (0.5) times Dusk. Noon (41 degrees) is unchanged (the share is 1 there); night still blends to the exposure-compensated moonlit haze; LOW's painted cloud ring takes the fog's colour, so it dims too. LOOK tab, SKY + HAZE: "Sunset haze dims with the light", "Sunset haze takes the sun's colour".
